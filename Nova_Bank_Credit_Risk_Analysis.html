1. Executive Overview

Nova Bank operates a loan portfolio of 32,581 accounts across the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada with a total exposure of $312.43 million. This analysis examines the credit risk profile of this portfolio to identify key default drivers, high-risk borrower segments, and geographic risk concentrations — with the aim of informing Nova Bank's lending policy and risk management strategy.

The overall default rate of 21.82% is materially high for a retail lending portfolio, with 7,108 borrowers having defaulted on their loans. Total exposure at default stands at $75.18 million — representing nearly a quarter of the entire portfolio. These figures indicate a significant credit risk challenge that warrants immediate attention across loan grading, borrower screening, and debt-to-income thresholds.

2. Key Findings
2.1 Loan Grade is the Strongest Default Predictor

Loan grade emerges as the single most powerful predictor of default. The relationship between grade and default rate is stark and consistent:

Grade A Grade B Grade C Grade D Grade E Grade F Grade G
9.96% 16.28% 20.73% 59.05% 64.42% 70.54% 98.44%

Grade G borrowers have a near-total default rate of 98.44%, making Grade G lending essentially non-viable. Grades E, F and G combined represent a severe concentration of default risk requiring immediate policy action.

2.2 Loan Purpose Drives Disproportionate Risk
  • Debt Consolidation: 28.59% default rate — highest of all loan purposes
  • Medical loans: 26.70% — borrowers already in financial distress at point of application
  • Home Improvement: 26.10% — above-average risk
  • Venture: 14.81% and Education: 17.22% — significantly safer

This pattern suggests borrowers seeking to consolidate debt or manage emergency expenses are inherently higher risk. Purpose-based risk differentiation should be applied more rigorously in underwriting.

2.3 Financial Ratios Predict Default

The DTI and LTI ratios are strong predictors of default. The scatter analysis shows a clear cluster of defaulted loans at higher DTI and LTI values. Portfolio breakdown by risk tier:

  • High Risk borrowers (DTI > 0.40): 22.7% of portfolio — default rate 44.32%
  • Moderate Risk (DTI 0.20–0.40): 54.8% of portfolio — default rate 14.82%
  • Safe borrowers (DTI < 0.20): 22.5% of portfolio — default rate 10.98%

Borrowers with prior defaults on file show a dramatically higher future default rate of 37.56% compared to 18.09% for those with no prior default history.

2.4 Loan Term and Borrower Age

Shorter loan terms consistently show lower default rates across all countries. 12-month loans default at 19.94% vs 22.34% for 60-month loans. Borrowers aged Above 60 show the highest default rate (28.4%), while those aged 36–45 show the lowest (17.8%).

4. Policy Recommendations
CRITICAL
Restrict or Eliminate Grade G and F Lending

With default rates of 98.44% and 70.54% respectively, Grade G and F loans are generating losses that almost certainly exceed any interest income. Nova Bank should implement an immediate review with a view to imposing strict caps or suspension pending a full risk pricing review.

HIGH PRIORITY
Tighten DTI Thresholds in Underwriting

Borrowers with DTI > 0.40 default at nearly 4× the rate of those with DTI < 0.20. Implement a hard DTI ceiling of 0.40 for new approvals, with enhanced due diligence for applicants between 0.35–0.40.

HIGH PRIORITY
Apply Purpose-Based Risk Pricing

Debt Consolidation and Medical loans carry materially higher default rates than Education and Venture loans. Apply risk-adjusted pricing based on loan purpose — higher interest rates or additional collateral for high-risk categories.

MEDIUM
Strengthen Prior Default Screening

Borrowers with a prior default on file default at 37.56% — nearly double the portfolio average. Implement enhanced screening including mandatory income verification, lower LTI caps and shorter initial loan terms.

MEDIUM
Favour Shorter Loan Terms

Shorter terms (12 months) consistently carry lower default rates than longer terms (60 months) across all three countries. Incentivise shorter repayment terms through preferential rates.

LOW
Maintain Current Geographic Strategy

Near-identical default rates across Canada, UK and USA confirm that geographic diversification is not introducing concentration risk. No country-specific adjustment is required, though volume monitoring in Ontario and London is recommended.

5. Conclusion

This analysis reveals that Nova Bank faces a significant and addressable credit risk challenge. The 21.82% default rate is driven primarily by concentrated risk in lower loan grades (E, F, G), high-DTI borrowers, and certain loan purposes — particularly debt consolidation. These are characteristics that can be screened, priced and managed through more rigorous underwriting standards.

The portfolio's geographic diversification across three countries is working as intended — risk is evenly distributed and no single market is disproportionately exposed. The challenge is therefore an internal underwriting and risk segmentation problem, not a market or macroeconomic one.

Implementing the recommendations in this report — particularly the DTI threshold tightening and Grade F/G restrictions — would be expected to meaningfully reduce the default rate over a 12–24 month horizon while preserving the portfolio's core growth trajectory in safer risk segments.
