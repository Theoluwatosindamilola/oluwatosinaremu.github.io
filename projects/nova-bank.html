1. Executive Overview

Nova Bank operates a loan portfolio of 32,581 accounts across the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada, with total exposure of $312.43M. This analysis examines the portfolio's credit risk profile to identify default drivers, high-risk borrower segments, and geographic concentration, with the aim of informing lending policy.

The overall default rate of 21.82% is high for a retail lending portfolio, with 7,108 borrowers in default and $75.18M at risk — nearly a quarter of the portfolio.

2. Key Findings

2.1 Loan Grade is the Strongest Default Predictor

Default rate rises sharply from Grade A (9.96%) through Grade G (98.44%), making Grade G lending essentially non-viable under current risk parameters. Grades E, F, and G combined represent a significant concentration of default risk.

2.2 Loan Purpose Drives Disproportionate Risk

Debt Consolidation loans carry the highest default rate (28.59%), followed by Medical (26.70%) and Home Improvement (26.10%) — suggesting these borrowers are often already in financial distress at application. Venture (14.81%) and Education (17.22%) loans are comparatively low-risk.

2.3 Financial Ratios Predict Default

  • High Risk (DTI > 0.40): 22.7% of portfolio — 44.32% default rate
  • Moderate Risk (DTI 0.20–0.40): 54.8% of portfolio — 14.82% default rate
  • Safe (DTI < 0.20): 22.5% of portfolio — 10.98% default rate

Borrowers with a prior default on file default again at 37.56%, versus 18.09% for those with no prior history.

2.4 Loan Term and Age Profile

Shorter loan terms carry lower default risk — 12-month loans default at 19.94% versus 22.34% for 60-month loans. Borrowers above 60 show the highest default rate (28.4%); those aged 36–45 show the lowest (17.8%).

2.5 Geographic Risk is Uniform

Default rates are near-identical across Canada (21.4%), UK (21.8%), and USA (22.3%) — indicating the root cause of default is borrower profile, not geography. Ontario and London carry the highest absolute default volumes due to borrower concentration.

3. Borrower Risk Segmentation

Borrowers were segmented into three tiers by DTI ratio:

  • Safe (DTI < 0.20): 4,016 borrowers, 10.98% default rate, avg income $86,770
  • Moderate (0.20–0.40): ~18K borrowers, 14.82% default rate, avg income $70,020
  • High Risk (DTI > 0.40): ~10K borrowers, 38.52% default rate, avg income $51,450

4. Policy Recommendations

CRITICALRestrict or eliminate Grade F/G lending — default rates of 70.54% and 98.44% almost certainly exceed any interest income generated.
HIGHTighten DTI thresholds — implement a hard ceiling of 0.40 for new approvals, with enhanced diligence for 0.35–0.40.
HIGHApply purpose-based risk pricing — higher rates or collateral requirements for Debt Consolidation and Medical loans.
MEDIUMStrengthen prior-default screening — mandatory income verification and lower LTI caps for applicants with prior defaults.
MEDIUMFavour shorter loan terms — incentivize 12-month terms through preferential rates.
LOWMaintain current geographic strategy — no country-specific adjustment needed; monitor volume in Ontario and London.

5. Conclusion

Nova Bank's 21.82% default rate is driven primarily by concentrated risk in lower loan grades, high-DTI borrowers, and certain loan purposes — all screenable and manageable through tighter underwriting. Geographic diversification is working as intended, with risk evenly distributed across markets. Implementing the recommendations above — particularly DTI tightening and Grade F/G restrictions — would be expected to meaningfully reduce the default rate over a 12–24 month horizon.
